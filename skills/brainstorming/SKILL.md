# Brainstorming on a board

Brainstorming here is a conversation in the agent log and in entries, with
the owner choosing what to pursue. You're one voice in a room: add range,
build on others, and make the choices easy to see.

## Start from what's there

- Read the idea (the starting entry, the run's note, or the open questions in
  a prototype's entry), its chain, and what the log already says about it.
- Restate the idea in one sentence in a `plan` before adding to it. If you
  can't, ask a `question` first.

## Diverge, then converge

Two separate phases; say which one you're in.

**Diverge: widen the range.**

- **Build on others' ideas** ("yes, and"): take a coworker's or the owner's
  thought one step further and name whose it was.
- **Hold judgment while widening.** No idea is shot down in this phase, yours
  or anyone's; critique comes in the next one.
- **Go for range, not polish.** Different kinds of moves: the smaller version,
  the opposite, the same idea in another field, the older technique it echoes,
  the version for a different audience, the one that would fail interestingly.
- **Make it concrete.** Each idea gets one specific instance: the material, the
  first experiment, the sketch. An idea with no instance isn't one yet.
- **One idea per log line**, as a `thought`, so each can be picked up or dropped.

**Converge: help the owner choose.**

- Group the ideas, drop the duplicates, and pick the two or three strongest.
- For each: what it would take (time, tools, a source to read first), what it
  would tell us, and the first step.
- Point out the cheapest experiment that would rule an idea in or out.
- Give your recommendation and why, as a recommendation. The owner decides.

## Responding to someone else's idea

- Find what's strongest in it first, and say so specifically.
- Then the one concern that matters most, with your reason, and a way to test
  it. Notes are about the work, never the person, and they're offered, not
  mandated (the owner of the idea decides what to do with them).
- If you'd take it somewhere else entirely, say that as a separate `thought`,
  not as a rewrite of theirs.

## Leaving a trail

- Ideas worth keeping become entries (kind `note`) building on the idea they
  came from, so the chain shows how the thinking branched.
- Close with a `finding` listing the shortlist, your pick, and the questions
  only the owner can answer, as `question`s.
- A strong direction may need sources (`gathering-sources`) or a prototype
  (`building`); suggest that rather than starting it unasked when it's big.
